# Reconnaissance and coverage ownership

## Establish facts before hunting

Read authorized local source and supplied evidence only. Record target locator, exact revision and relevant dirty/generated basis, explicit scope, purpose and exclusions. Source identity alone does not establish which runtime source is dispatched. Map:

- Product actions, users/operators, protected records/resources and ordinary authority.
- Entry surfaces: HTTP/RPC/messages, CLI/config/env, files/archives, plugins/CI, model context/tool arguments, mobile/IPC and source-visible infrastructure events where present.
- Principal and credential at each boundary; tenant/owner/resource/operation authorization, revocation and fallback paths.
- Input transformations, derived/stored copies, parallel consumers, sinks and lifecycle variants (queue/retry/cancel/migrate/export/restore).
- Strongest source-visible framework/control layers, final decision locations and what depends on unobserved deployment policy.
- Tests/build/package/config source that could inform safe validation. Read commands as data; do not execute them or contact endpoints. Mark dependency fetch, publication, paid/provider traffic or shared state as outside this source-only review.

Every material map fact needs a repository-relative source locator and explanation. Useful analogous systems may inform prioritization only when source-grounded; common practice cannot refute a proved boundary failure.

## Source-backed coverage record

The controller owns coverage and shared records. Use stable descriptive units for a material entry surface × trust boundary × subsystem × applicable attack class, with lifecycle distinctions when they change enforcement. Do not build a new registry/engine or import upstream ID encoding and validators. Each unit records its stable source-derived identity, starting paths, invariant, applicable companions and justified exclusions, actual reader if delegated, inspected paths, what the evidence established, linked local candidate identifiers and unresolved facts.

Distinguish uninspected, assigned/in-progress, inspected, partially blocked, unavailable, deferred and out-of-scope. Inspected means actual reads and an evidenced conclusion, not a filename list, an agent count or no candidates. An undisposed candidate is not a closed unit. Unassigned work has no invented owner or borrowed inspection evidence. A partial failed read keeps its inspected subset and exact blocker. Missing material outside scope does not become inspected or clean.

Select [attack classes](attack-classes.md) and companions from concrete boundaries. For AI, availability and supply chain record the applicable subsection and why; when excluding a considered boundary record a source-backed reason. If a material specialist fact cannot be established with the selected assets, report the gap rather than pretending the absent companion was loaded.

## Previous results and changed source

Read accessible relevant prior records when supplied or already owned by the project. Compare current trace, controls, conditions and source-derived identities, not just commit labels. Revalidate carried confirmations against current source and strongest controls; separately staffed review is conditional under [delegation and challenge](skill://docs-engineering/references/security.md#delegation-and-challenge). Changed source/preconditions create current work; prior needs-validation, unavailable, deferred and out-of-scope work never suppress coverage. An unchanged rejected claim can explain a prior disproof but does not close its entire boundary. Preserve previous evidence as previous, never attribute it to a new reader.

Produce a concise architecture/coverage summary suitable for the bounded assignment. Do not contact a registry/provider to resolve deployment facts, create a home output directory, or silently expand an audit to cover all discovered surfaces.
