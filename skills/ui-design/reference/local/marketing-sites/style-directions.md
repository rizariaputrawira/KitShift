# Optional style directions

Load only when the brief or established brand supports a named direction below. These are descriptive starting points, not a randomizer, palette library, or mandatory design system. Adapt them to the actual content, assets, installed stack, identity, accessibility needs, and requested scope. If no direction is requested or evidenced, do not select one on the user's behalf.

## `minimalist-editorial`
A restrained editorial approach can use clear typographic hierarchy, deliberate whitespace, and quiet structural separation to keep attention on real content. Restraint does not require a warm monochrome palette, serif type, flat cards, or omission of useful imagery and interaction. Choose only details that serve this project's voice and readability.

## `industrial-print`
An industrial-print direction may draw on Swiss-grid organization, strong typographic contrast, visible rules, and print-like hierarchy. Use the grid to clarify real information and relationships, not to manufacture labels or simulated operational data. Palette, type, density, and corner treatment come from the brief and brand, not a fixed recipe.

## `tactical-crt`
A tactical/CRT direction may use terminal-inspired typography and framing or restrained display effects when those choices fit the product and audience. Do not invent telemetry, statuses, identifiers, or data. Effects such as scanlines, phosphor color, noise, or distortion are optional, not required; keep content legible, contrast sufficient, and motion nonessential. Provide a usable reduced-motion experience and avoid effects that obscure controls or text.

## `high-end-editorial`
A spacious editorial composition can use considered hierarchy, pacing, and responsive relationships between text and genuine media. It does not require luxury tropes, a particular typeface or palette, oversized empty space, bevels, or motion. Preserve the actual brand and content; use only the space and media that help the reader.

Across all directions, do not treat these names as permission to copy a reference brand, introduce unsupported claims/assets, or override user instructions. Test responsive collapse and legibility in the actual implementation; no direction prescribes a fixed layout or numerical design token.
