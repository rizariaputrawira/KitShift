# Landing pages and portfolios: brief-led composition

Use this reference for a requested landing page, portfolio, or related marketing surface. The brief, audience, existing brand, and real content determine the design. This is a way to reason about composition, not a prescribed page structure or style.

## Read before composing

- Identify the page's actual purpose and audience: what should a visitor understand or do, and what information supports that decision?
- Read the relevant project components, design guidance, copy, routes, and assets. Preserve established identity and useful patterns unless the brief asks for a change.
- Note constraints such as accessibility, regulated claims, localization, performance, and narrow viewports. These constrain visual choices rather than being traded away for them.
- Resolve material ambiguity with one concise question when context cannot answer it. Otherwise state the working interpretation briefly and proceed.

## Compose from actual content

Build hierarchy around the content and evidence that exist. Choose only sections needed to explain the offering, present selected work, answer real questions, or support a requested action. Structure may be editorial, compact, image-led, typographic, or another fit for this project; no standard hero, feature grid, testimonial, pricing, or CTA sequence is required.

Use supplied and authorized assets. Do not invent customer names, quotes, logos, product screens, metrics, credentials, claims, or image URLs. Do not use placeholder imagery or generated assets as if they depict the real product or people. If an asset is necessary and unavailable, say what is missing; if imagery is not necessary, do not add it for decoration. Preserve factual meaning when tightening copy and identify unverified claims rather than presenting them as fact.

Use the actual installed framework, styling approach, components, tokens, and dependencies. Do not migrate the stack, add a library, or imitate another brand's proprietary system merely because a reference resembles it. Treat reference sites as evidence about a requested quality or interaction, not permission to copy their assets or identity.

## Check the delivered composition

At the widths and states relevant to the request, ensure content order and reading remain clear, text and controls fit, meaningful media has appropriate alternatives, and interactive elements remain operable by keyboard and touch. Avoid layout choices that hide content, force horizontal overflow, or depend on hover alone. Verify factual copy against its supplied source. Report missing inputs and unverified behavior plainly.
